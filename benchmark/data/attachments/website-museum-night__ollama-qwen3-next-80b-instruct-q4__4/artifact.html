Concept

ARTIFACT: Museum Night is a curated experience that transforms the museum into a living, breathing canvas after hours. Through immersive art, live soundscapes, and hidden narratives, guests are invited to explore the unseen stories behind the collection — where silence speaks louder than words, and every shadow holds a secret.

After-hours Exhibitions

Experience the permanent collection in a new light — illuminated only by curated spotlights, with no crowds, no noise, just the art and your thoughts.

Live Performances

Immerse yourself in ambient music, spoken word, and experimental theatre performed by leading artists in unexpected gallery spaces.

Guided Hidden-Room Tours

Discover rooms usually closed to the public — restored archives, artist studios, and secret corridors only accessible during Museum Night.

Elena Marquez

Sound Sculptor

Performance: 21:00, Sculpture Courtyard

Elena Marquez transforms museum artifacts into resonant instruments, blending historical objects with contemporary sound design. Her work explores the sonic memory embedded in metal, glass, and stone. She has exhibited at the Venice Biennale and the Tate Modern, and her installations have been featured in The New Yorker and Artforum.

Kai Tanaka

Projection Designer

Performance: 22:00, Sculpture Hall

Kai Tanaka is a digital artist specializing in historical narrative projection. He uses AI-assisted analysis of museum collections to animate classical sculptures with stories drawn from letters, diaries, and archival records. His work has been commissioned by the Louvre and the Smithsonian, and he is known for creating immersive experiences that challenge the boundaries between past and present.

Rosa D'Angelo

Curator

Tour: 20:00, Storage Wing

Rosa D'Angelo is the Chief Curator of The Old Gallery and the architect of Museum Night. She specializes in uncovering suppressed narratives within museum collections, particularly those of women, immigrants, and marginalized communities. Her groundbreaking exhibitions have redefined how institutions engage with contested histories. She holds a PhD in Cultural Anthropology from the Sorbonne.

Dmitri Volkov

Performance Artist

Performance: Midnight Gallery Walk

Dmitri Volkov is a pioneer of silent performance art. His work involves moving through museum spaces with extreme stillness, creating an atmosphere of contemplative presence. He believes that the most powerful art is the one that exists in the space between the observer and the object. He has performed at the Guggenheim, MoMA, and the National Gallery in London.

Aisha Nkosi

Installation Artist

Installation: All Night

Aisha Nkosi creates multi-sensory environments using scent, light, and texture to evoke emotional memory. Her work at Museum Night will feature a series of "memory chambers" infused with archival fragrances — old paper, dried flowers, and museum dust — designed to trigger personal recollections in visitors. Her installations have been exhibited in the Centre Pompidou and the Tate Modern.

Julien Moreau

Spoken Word Poet

Performance: 23:00, Lecture Hall

Julien Moreau is a poet whose work draws from museum archives and personal histories. His performance tonight will weave together letters from 19th-century collectors, anonymous donor notes, and fragments from museum staff diaries. His poetry collection "The Quiet Rooms" was shortlisted for the Pulitzer Prize. He lives in Paris and teaches creative writing at the Sorbonne.

Fernanda Silva

Lighting Director

Lighting Design: All Night

Fernanda Silva designs lighting that tells stories without words. Her work at Museum Night uses dynamic spotlights and ambient glows to highlight the emotional weight of each artifact. She believes light is the first language of museums. Her designs have been featured in the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Victoria and Albert Museum. She is a professor at the Royal College of Art in London.

Yuki Hara

Composer

Performance: 01:00, Archive Room

Yuki Hara composes music using field recordings from museum spaces — the echo of footsteps in empty halls, the creak of wooden cabinets, the whisper of fabric on display cases. Her score for Museum Night is an ambient meditation on time, silence, and memory. She has collaborated with the Berlin Philharmonic and released two albums on Deutsche Grammophon. She lives in Kyoto and spends her days listening to the sounds of ancient temples.

FAQ

Is payment processed on this page?
No, this is a demonstration page. No actual payment is processed. All reservations are simulated for event planning purposes only.
Can I arrive late?
Yes, entry is permitted until 23:00. However, some experiences like the Hidden Collection Tour and Artist Talks may have limited capacity after their start time.
Are photos allowed?
Yes, non-flash photography is permitted in all public areas. Flash photography and tripods are prohibited to preserve the atmosphere and protect the artworks.
Is the venue accessible?
Yes, the entire venue is fully wheelchair accessible with elevators to all floors. Wheelchair rentals are available at the entrance, and sign language interpreters can be arranged with 48 hours' notice.
Can I change my ticket tier?
Yes, you can upgrade your ticket up to 24 hours before the event. Downgrades are not permitted. Contact us at info@artifactmuseumnight.com for assistance.
How do I save the event to my calendar?
Click the "Save to Calendar" button in the hero section. A .ics file will download automatically. Open it with your calendar app (Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, Outlook, etc.) to add the event.
